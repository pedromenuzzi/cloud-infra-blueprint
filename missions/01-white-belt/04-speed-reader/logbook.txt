Day 1: Set sail from Ushuaia aboard the icebreaker Kernel Star.
Day 2: Rough seas. The coffee machine is the only casualty so far.
Day 3: First iceberg spotted. It looks suspiciously like a giant penguin.
Day 4: Crossed the Antarctic Circle. Celebrated with lukewarm cocoa.
Day 5: Engine hiccup fixed by turning it off and on again. Classic.
Day 6: A pod of orcas escorted the ship for two hours.
Day 7: Reached the pack ice. Progress slows to a crawl.
Day 8: Emperor penguins sighted on the port side. Morale at maximum.
Day 9: Set up base camp on the ice shelf. Tents holding steady.
Day 10: Radio check with mainland: all systems nominal.
Day 11: Weather station assembled. Wind at 30 knots.
Day 12: First scientific samples collected from the ice core.
Day 13: A curious penguin inspected our supply crates. Approved, apparently.
Day 14: Whiteout conditions. Nobody leaves the tents today.
Day 15: Storm passed. Solar panels dug out of the snow.
Day 16: Long march to the eastern ridge. Legs are done.
Day 17: Ice cave discovered. Walls glow a deep glacier blue.
Day 18: Sample analysis running on the field laptop overnight.
Day 19: Aurora australis danced across the sky for an hour.
Day 20: Generator maintenance day. Grease everywhere.
Day 21: Sled dog Marmite refused to work before breakfast. Related to us, clearly.
Day 22: Mapped two kilometres of the crevasse field.
Day 23: Hot soup. Best meal of the entire expedition so far.
Day 24: Repaired a torn tent seam before the next storm cycle.
Day 25: Ice core drill reached 40 metres. New record for the team.
Day 26: Strange radio interference logged in the evening.
Day 27: SIGNAL: aurora-borealis-7 detected on the northern ridge.
Day 28: Triangulating the signal source. Coordinates logged.
Day 29: Signal source turned out to be our own beacon. Facepalm.
Day 30: Rest day. Chess tournament: penguins 3, humans 1.
Day 31: Wind turbine back online after bearing swap.
Day 32: Photographed a leopard seal from a safe distance.
Day 33: Inventory check: enough biscuits for three more weeks.
Day 34: Marked the halfway point of the expedition.
Day 35: Collected atmospheric data through the night.
Day 36: A snow bridge collapsed behind the sled. Too close.
Day 37: Backup batteries rotated and recharged.
Day 38: Final samples sealed and labelled for transport.
Day 39: Began packing the base camp. Bittersweet.
Day 40: Farewell visit from the emperor penguin colony.
Day 41: Boarded the Kernel Star for the journey home.
Day 42: Course set north. The ice fades behind us. End of log.
